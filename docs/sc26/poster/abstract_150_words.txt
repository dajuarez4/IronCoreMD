Earth's inner core reaches pressures and temperatures that make direct experimental study difficult. Because iron is its dominant component, resolving the stability of its body-centered-cubic, face-centered-cubic, and hexagonal-close-packed phases is essential for interpreting seismic observations and planetary evolution. We combine first-principles density functional theory, ab initio molecular dynamics, and temperature-dependent effective potential analysis to construct a consistent dataset of iron structures, energies, forces, pressures, and vibrational properties. To extend these calculations to larger systems and longer time scales, we are developing a Gaussian-process interatomic model based on graph-kernel structural similarity. The current graph-kernel benchmark gives an energy MAE of 7.26 meV/atom and RMSE of 9.61 meV/atom on 156 evaluation configurations. We validate beyond energy parity using force errors, equation-of-state behavior, phonon stability, magnetic sensitivity, and free-energy trends across core-relevant conditions.
