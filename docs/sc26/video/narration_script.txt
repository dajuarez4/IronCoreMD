Do you know what happens more than five thousand kilometers beneath our feet?

Deep inside Earth's core, iron is exposed to extreme pressures and temperatures—conditions that are nearly impossible to observe directly.

To explore this hidden environment, we use high-performance computing to perform first-principles Density Functional Theory calculations. These quantum-mechanical simulations allow us to study the structure, energy, and stability of different forms of iron under core-like conditions.

However, these calculations are computationally expensive and limited to small systems and short time scales.

Our solution is to train a machine-learning interatomic potential using data generated from these simulations.

We represent atomic structures as graphs, allowing graph kernels to identify similarities between different atomic environments and learn the energy landscape.

Our model reproduces important phase-stability and equation-of-state trends, with energy errors ranging from four to nine millielectronvolts per atom.

Can machine learning help us simulate larger systems and uncover the dynamic behavior of iron inside Earth?
