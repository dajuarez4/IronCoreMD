# SC26 One-Minute Cartoon Storyboard

## Core message

DFT provides the accuracy needed to study iron inside Earth, while graph-kernel machine learning provides the scale needed to reach larger systems and longer time scales.

## Timed scenes

| Time | Cartoon scene | Motion and callouts | Narration cue |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–8 s | A clean cartoon Earth in space; the camera dives through the mantle toward a glowing inner core. | Slow zoom. Add only one label: **Earth's inner core**. | “More than five thousand kilometers beneath our feet…” |
| 8–17 s | The core becomes a field of iron atoms. Three small clusters rearrange into BCC, FCC, and HCP structures. | Morph or cross-fade between the three lattices. | “Iron dominates this region…” |
| 17–26 s | A supercomputer calculates one small atomic cell while a clock and cost meter accelerate. | Show the cell staying small while time and cost increase. Callout: **DFT: accurate, expensive**. | “First-principles simulations can answer this…” |
| 26–38 s | The atomic structure transforms into a network graph. Two graphs are compared and connected by a glowing similarity line. | Animate atoms becoming nodes and bonds becoming edges. Callout: **Graph similarity**. | “Our solution is to turn each iron structure into a graph…” |
| 38–48 s | Zoom into the poster's graph-kernel parity plot. Highlight the diagonal and the **9.61 meV/atom RMSE** metric. | Use a circle or arrow; do not show the entire poster yet. | “Our current model reaches an error…” |
| 48–56 s | Transition to the magnetic/non-magnetic phonon plot. The two curves move together while small callouts appear: forces, phonons, free energy, magnetism. | Highlight that validation extends beyond energy. | “We then challenge it with physics…” |
| 56–60 s | Reveal the full UTEP poster with title and QR-code area. | Slow push-in. Final text: **Explore the full poster**. | “The goal is simple…” |

## Visual style

- Friendly scientific cartoon, not childish.
- UTEP orange, dark brown, white, and a small amount of blue.
- Flat 2D shapes with subtle depth and smooth camera movement.
- Large labels: no more than six words on screen at once.
- Use the actual plots for scenes 5 and 6 so the video remains scientifically connected to the poster.
- Avoid paragraphs, equations, rapidly moving backgrounds, and a full technical walkthrough.
